test: a cusp row is its orbits, admitted as the walk draws them

`cusps` yields one representative per orbit, so its row holds distinct orbits
and its enumeration needs no table of its points: the row is admitted as it is
produced, its inverse is the cusps' own identity, and the cusps' equality
answers for a candidate that is not one of them.  This gates the two statements
that admission rests on, on the four specimens a test call can afford: the
kernel plane and line rows of `U + U(2) + <-2>` (two and three cusps) and of
`U + U(2) + <-4>` (three and four), the full orthogonal group's line row of the
second lattice (two), and `E10`'s empty kernel plane row.

The pairwise distinctness is the obligation the admission takes on: it no longer
identifies a repetition, so a walk that yielded one orbit twice would leave it
standing in the row twice, and that is what this asserts absent, one question a
pair.  The rest is the inverse: each cusp answers its own position, is admitted
by the row, and names a primitive totally isotropic rank-`rank` sublattice whose
own membership in the cusp holds.

Inert for the timing defect that row's ingress carries, said here rather than
manufactured red.  A test that failed without the repair would have to be red by
time on a row where the ingress dominates the build, and such a row is at least
29 plane cusps against the 20 s ceiling and 49 against the 60 s hang bound, at
50.6 ms a pair, while the rows a test call can afford are 1-9 cusps whose
ingress is 0.1-0.5 s of a 33-88 s build; a red by counting is not available
either, the test lint banning mocks, `id`, and the bypass constructors, which is
where the ingress lives.  The measurements are in
`results/issue598-the-cusp-rows-ingress-is-linear.md`.

Runs: this file on the repaired tree, 5 passed in 21.32 s, slowest call 10.64 s
(the kernel plane row of `U + U(2) + <-2>`, which is the subgroup construction);
on the parent `61b3fa1`, 5 passed in 20.49 s -- so the file does not see the
defect, it sees the contract.
