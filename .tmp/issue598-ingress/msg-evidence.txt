evidence: the cusp rows' ingress before and after, at 100 to 100000 planes

`results/issue598-the-cusp-rows-ingress-is-linear.md` is the report:
what the literal ingress costs a cusp row, the linear form and how it differs
from the one `tits_building_incidence` uses, the element-by-element comparison of
the two admissions over one list of 100 plane cusps of `Otilde(T_Co)`, the curve
at 1 000 / 10 000 / 100 000 planes, and why a red test for the timing cannot fit
the call ceiling.

`.tmp/issue598-ingress/` holds every probe, launcher and log.  The numbers on
this host (adopted runtime Sage 11.0.beta5, port `991b88b0`):

- one list of 100 plane cusps, both admissions: 249.986 s against under a
  millisecond, with the two rows the same point by point, the same spans, the
  same inverse `0..99`, and 100 distinct spans among the 100 cusps;
- 1 000 / 10 000 / 100 000 planes: 1.77 / 2.11 / 2.11 us a plane at admission on
  a flat per-plane build (6.86 / 6.47 / 6.79 ms);
- 527 line cusps of the same subgroup: 245.2 s of walk, the old ingress 11.1--12.0
  ms a pair on top of it;
- the rows of four small lattices, 16 rows over {O(L), Otilde(L)} x {rank 1,
  rank 2}, identical counts before and after; and the same speculation that the
  row is not a sequence across processes -- two runs of one tree differ in the
  order of the orbits and in which representative each retains -- which is why
  the element-by-element comparison is made on one list in one process.

`tests/lattices/test_scattone_plane_orbits.sage` measured 85.37, 32.22 and 26.72
s for three of its five tests against the 20 s ceiling on the parent tree, which
is recorded because it is pre-existing and because it rules those lattices out as
specimens for a new gate test.

Not measured: the row's own 189720 planes (the transversal plus 1288 s of planes
plus the admission is 25 minutes, and the build was measured to 100000); the
line row's full admission, which was not run because 139351 pairs at 11.4 ms is
26 minutes on top of the 245 s walk.
