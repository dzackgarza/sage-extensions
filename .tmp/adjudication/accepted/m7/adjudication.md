## H1 — exact embedding: **PROVED** (and the quoted hypothesis fails for N)

**(a) The scaling map is an isometry onto an index-2, non-primitive sublattice.**
`U(2) = [[0,2],[2,0]] → U = [[0,1],[1,0]]`, `(y₁,y₂) ↦ (2y₁, y₂)`: the image is `⟨2e,f⟩` with
`(2e,f) = 2`, `(2e,2e) = (f,f) = 0`, i.e. Gram = U(2); index `[U:⟨2e,f⟩] = |det| = 2`
(S1 of the transcript).  It is **not primitive**: `2e ∈ ⟨2e,f⟩` but `e ∉ ⟨2e,f⟩`.
Hence `N = U ⊕ U(2) ⊕ E8(-2) ⊂ M := U ⊕ U ⊕ E8(-2)` with index 2,
`M/N = ⟨ē₂⟩ ≅ Z/2`, and N is not primitive in M (`2e₂ ∈ N`, `e₂ ∉ N`, S2).  This is exactly
line 1518–1520's embedding; the text does not record that its index is 2 or that it is improper.

**(b) Duals and discriminant maps.**  `N^∨ = U ⊕ ⟨e₂, f₂/2⟩ ⊕ L^∨` (S2: `e₂, f₂/2 ∈ N^∨`,
`e₁/2, e₂/2 ∉ N^∨`); `M^∨ = U ⊕ U ⊕ L^∨` since `U⊕U` is unimodular.  Therefore
`A_N = N^∨/N ≅ A_{U(2)} ⊕ A_{E8(-2)} ≅ (Z/2)² ⊕ (Z/2)⁸ = (Z/2)¹⁰`,
`A_M = M^∨/M = A_L ≅ (Z/2)⁸`, and the chain `N ⊂ M ⊂ N^∨` has
`[M:N] = [N^∨:M^∨] = 2`, `[N^∨:M] = 2`, i.e. the exact sequences
`0 → A_M → A_N → A_N/A_M → 0` (quotient of order 4) and `0 → M/N → A_N`.
Concretely `A_{U(2)} = ⟨[e₂], [f₂/2]⟩` with `q = (0,0,0,1)` and `b([e₂],[f₂/2]) = ½` (S2) — the
split 2-dimensional form; the class of `e₂` is the element of `A_N` represented by `M`.
No half-integrality arises *from N-vectors* in the `U(2)`-directions: `(N^∨, e) ⊂ Z` for every
`e ∈ N` (N^∨ is the dual of N), and for the ambient vector `e₂ ∈ M \ N` one has
`(e₂, N^∨) = 2Z`; the half-integral lattice is `N^∨`, not N.

**(c) Sharpening — the quoted lemma's class contains no isomorph of N.**  `A_N` is elementary
abelian of rank **10**, so it needs 10 generators.  If `N ≅ U ⊕ U ⊕ L` then `rank L = 8` and
`A_L = A_{U⊕U⊕L}` is generated by the 8 vectors of a dual basis of L, so it needs ≤ 8
generators.  Contradiction.  Hence **N is isomorphic to no lattice of the form `U+U+L`**
(S3); the same argument shows `K = U(2) ⊕ E8(-2)` splits off no `U`.  So Lemma/Prop 3.7.3
(1429, 1448) — a statement about lattices *of the form* `U+U+L` — **does not apply to N itself**;
its only route to N is through the improper index-2 inclusion of (a), which is precisely what the
quoted text asserts "needs no additional work" (1519–1520).

**Verdict H1: proved.**  The embedding exists, is isometric, of index 2, non-primitive; the
discriminant maps are as computed; and no isomorph of N satisfies the quoted hypothesis.

## H2 — which subgroup is induced: **REFUTED — the descended group leaves the kernel**

**Lemma A (the in-N sub-family is in the kernel).**  Let `e,x ∈ N` with `e` isotropic and
`x ∈ e^⊥`.  Then `E_{e,x}(y) = y + (y,e)x − (y,x)e − ½(x,x)(y,e)e` is an isometry of N acting
trivially on `A_N`.
*Proof.*  For `y ∈ N^∨`: `(y,e), (y,x) ∈ Z` (e,x ∈ N), `(x,x)/2 ∈ Z` (N even), so the displacement
is an integral combination of `e,x ∈ N`; `(e,e) = (e,x) = 0` gives isometry; the quoted identity
1446–1447 is exactly the composition law and holds for all `x,y ∈ e^⊥` (verified symbolically).
∎  (Verified numerically too, S6.)

**The gap.**  Lines 1448–1450 do not take `x ∈ N`: they take the `x`'s to be a Z-basis of
`e_i^⊥` *in the ambient lattice* `U+U+L`.  Integrality on N is weaker than `x ∈ N`, and the
`x` with N-external components are exactly where triviality breaks.  Explicit counterexample,
verified in exact arithmetic (`witness_H2.py`):

  model `M = U ⊕ U ⊕ ⟨−4⟩ ⊕ ⟨−4⟩` (rank 6, even, `M` unimodular on `U⊕U`), `N = U ⊕ U(2) ⊕ ⟨−4⟩²`,
  `e = e₂` (one of the four canonical isotropic vectors), `x = e₂ + l₁ ∈ e₂^⊥ ∩ M`.

  * `E := E_{e₂,e₂+l₁}` is an isometry of the rational space and **preserves** N:
    `f₂ ↦ 2e₂ + f₂ + l₁`, `l₁ ↦ 4e₂ + l₁`, `2e₂ ↦ 2e₂`, `e₁,f₁,l₂` fixed.  So `E ∈ O(M) ∩ O(N)`
    and it is one of the quoted generators.
  * `E` acts **trivially on `A_M = A_L`** (as it must, cf. 1457): every element of a dual basis of
    M is displaced by an element of M, e.g. `l₁/4 ↦ l₁/4 + e₂`.
  * `E` acts **non-trivially on `A_N`**: `E(f₂/2) − f₂/2 = e₂ + l₁/2 ∉ N`.

So `E' ⊄ ṼO(N)`; the "acts trivially on the ambient discriminant" of line 1457 does **not**
survive the descent (1518–1520's "no additional work is needed" is where it fails).

**What remains true, and why my earlier containment claim was wrong.**  Block maps (fixing L
pointwise) are safe: Lemma B of my first submission — the stabiliser of `N4` in `SL₂(Z)²` is
`{A : β even}` and every such element acts trivially on `A_{N4}` — is correct and verified
(S4–S5); the failure needs an `x` with an L-direction.  My Lemma A is correct but its hypothesis
is `x ∈ N`, and the quoted family is larger.  The mechanism is visible in the discriminants:
`[e₂] ∈ A_N` is represented by an element of `M \ N` (`e₂ ∉ N`), so triviality on `A_M` controls
only the subgroup `M/N ⊂ A_N`, not the second coset; the class `[f₂/2]` lives in that coset and
is moved by `[e₂]`.

**Verdict H2: refuted.**  The subgroup induced by DH's descent is the stabiliser
`S = O(M) ∩ O(N) ∩ {trivial on A_M}` (Alg 1), and it is **not** contained in
`ker(O(N) → O(A_N))`; the correct in-N sub-family (Lemma A) is.

## H3 — equality with `ker(O⁺(N) → O(A_N)) = ṼO(N) ∩ O⁺(N)`: **REFUTED**

Immediate from H2: `E'` contains `E_{e₂,e₂+l₁}|_N ∉ ṼO(N)`, hence `E' ≠ ṼO(N) ∩ O⁺(N)`; the
quoted group does not even consist of discriminant-trivial isometries of N.

Independent witness, of the other kind (elements of the kernel that the recipe cannot reach):
`g := id_{U⊕U(2)} ⊕ (−1)_{E8(-2)}` lies in `ṼO⁺(N)` (for `v ∈ N^∨`, `g(v)−v = −2·(L^∨-part) ∈ 2L^∨ = L`;
det = 1; `θ_A = 2⁸/2⁸ = 1`), while `−1_N`, `−1_{U(2)}` and `Γ_N(2) = ker(O(N) → O(N/2N))` are
further kernel elements (the last: `g(x) − x ∈ 2N` for `x ∈ N` gives `2(g(v)−v) = g(2v)−2v ∈ 2N`,
so `g(v)−v ∈ N` for `v ∈ N^∨`).  Whether these lie in `E'` is open (see the board's finding f8:
my attempted invariant fails for products, `E_{f1,r}E_{e1,r}(r) = 2e₁+4f₁+3r`), but it no longer
matters for the verdict — `E' ≠ ker` is already settled by H2.

**The obstruction candidates named in the question, settled.**
* (a) *`(e_i,N^∨) = ½Z` on `U(2)`-summands*: for `e ∈ N` one has `(N^∨,e) ⊂ Z`, so this does not
  occur inside N; the half-integral lattice is `N^∨` (`N^∨ = U ⊕ ⟨e₂, f₂/2⟩ ⊕ L^∨`).  The real
  source of half-integrality is `x ∉ N`, which is (H2).
* (b) *"does `F(e,x)` with `x ∈ e^⊥ ∩ N`, e in the `U(2)` factor, act trivially on `A_N`?"*:
  yes (Lemma A; the only isotropic vectors of N from the `U(2)` block are `f₂`, `2e₂`, and
  `e₂ ∉ N`), verified S6.  Again the failure needs `x ∉ N`.
* (c) *2-elementary / length*: no isomorph of N is `U+U+L` (§H1c); so the quoted theorem applies
  only to the improper overlattice, which is where the `x ∉ N` vectors enter.
* (d) *reflections*: `r_v` with `(v,v) = ±2`, `r_v ∈ O(N)`, is in `ṼO(N)` (as in Lemma A);
  but `r_{2e₂+f₂}` (norm 4) keeps `N4` and moves `[f₂/2]` (S7), so norm-±4 reflections are outside
  the kernel and the "±2, ±4 reflection" fallback is refuted.

**Verdict H3: refuted** — for two independent reasons: the quoted descended group already
contains an element outside the kernel (H2), and the kernel contains elements of a shape the
quoted generators cannot produce (`−1_{E8(-2)} ⊕ id`, `Γ_N(2)`).

## H4 — spinor norms: **PROVED** (exact values; both conventions given)

Conventions.  With `q(v) = (v,v)/2` put `θ_A(r_v) = q(v)`, so
`θ_A(g) = ∏ q(v_i)` over a reflection factorisation; `θ_B := det·θ_A` is the other normalisation.
The computation (`verify_m7.py`, S8) uses `θ_A(g) ≡ det((1+g)/2)` when `det(1+g) ≠ 0`, validated
on 200 random products of two reflections against the known value `q(v)q(w)` (196 agreements,
0 disagreements, 4 skipped), and multiplicativity spot-checked.  The **vendored text's own
convention is θ_B**: line 320–321 says a reflection in a length-2 vector of the summand `U` has
real spinor norm **−1**, and indeed for such a vector `det = −1`, `θ_A = +1`, so `θ_B = −1`
(verified: `r_{e₁+f₁}` has det −1 and `θ_A = 1`, S8).

| named generator | det | θ_A (mod squares) | in O⁺(θ_A) | in O⁺(θ_B) = vendored |

| `E_{e,x}`, e isotropic, e,x ∈ N | +1 | 1 (square, >0) | yes | **yes** |

**Verdict H4: proved** for every named generator, with the convention of the quoted text

## Sharpest alternative maintained statement, and what remains open

**Maintained theorem (proved here).**  For `N = U ⊕ U(2) ⊕ E8(-2)` (or any even lattice),
`ṼO(N) = ker(O(N) → O(A_N))` contains
1. every Eichler transvection `E_{e,x}` with `e ∈ N` isotropic, `x ∈ e^⊥ ∩ N` (Lemma A);
2. every reflection `r_v` with `v ∈ N`, `(v,v) = ±2`, `r_v ∈ O(N)` (Lemma A′);
3. `−1_{U⊕U(2)}`, `−1_{E8(-2)}`, `−1_N` (and `−1` on any even summand with 2-elementary A),
   together with `Γ_N(2) = ker(O(N) → O(N/2N))`;
4. every isometry of the overlattice `M = U⊕U⊕E8(-2)` that preserves N **and fixes the
   E8(-2)-summand pointwise** (Lemma B; verified for the whole stabiliser in the rank-4 block,
   S4–S5).  *Not* every N-preserving isometry of M: `E_{e₂,e₂+l₁}` is an N-preserving isometry of
   M, trivial on `A_M`, that is outside `ṼO(N)` (H2, `witness_H2.py`).
It does **not** contain the reflections in vectors of norm ±4 that preserve N (explicit
`r_{2e₂+f₂}`, S7), nor the transvections `E_{e,x}` with `x ∈ e^⊥ ∩ (M \ N)` (H2).

**Where a genuine generation theorem would have to come from** (not proved here, do not quote as
proved): `ṼO⁺(N)` needs, beyond the above, generators acting on the `E8(-2)`-block — at the very
least `−1_{E8(-2)}`; the natural candidate is `W(E8)` together with the level-2 congruence data,
which is exactly what the source itself adds at 1459–1461 ("adding the isometries of the E8
component … reduced to 3 representatives", the 3 W(E8)-orbits on E8/2E8 of sizes 1, 120, 135).
Expected generator count: the quoted recipe gives 4n = 48 for n = 12 (1451–1452), and it is
wrong in both directions — some of those 48 (the `E_{e_i,v}` with `v` having an N-external
component) leave `ṼO(N)` (H2), and the rest cannot reach `−1_{E8(-2)} ⊕ id` or `Γ_N(2)` (H3).
The minimal repair is to replace `x` ranging over `e_i^⊥` in the ambient lattice by
`x ∈ e_i^⊥ ∩ N` (Lemma A's hypothesis) and to add generators acting on the E8(-2)-block, at least
`−1_{E8(-2)}`; the source itself adds `W(E8)` (1459–1461).  No statement about `U+U+L`-shaped
lattices can be the right frame (H1c).

**Explicitly unproved / unverifiable from here.**  (i) Scattone's Proposition 3.7.3, §3.7,
Example 3.7.2 themselves (only the paraphrase at 1437–1454 is available).  (ii) That the stabiliser
`S` of 1468 generates `ṼO⁺(N)` — nothing in the source says so, and §H2 shows `S` is not even
contained in `ṼO(N)`.  (iii) A minimal generating set of

## §5 consistency check of the repository's measured |O(A_N)| (not part of H1–H4)

`A_N ≅ (Z/2)¹⁰` with `q = q_{U(2)} ⊕ q_{E8(-2)}`; the 2-dim block is split
(q-values 0,0,0,1 — computed, S2) and by (E2) the 8-dim block is the plus form, so `A_N` is of

    |O₁₀⁻(2)| = 2·2²⁰·33·3·15·63·255 = 50030759116800  ≠ 46998591897600.

So the repository's measured value is reproduced **independently**, and it pins the type of `A_N`
to plus — consistent with 1202–1206 ("O(N)/ṼO(N) ≅ O⁺(F₂) is finite").
