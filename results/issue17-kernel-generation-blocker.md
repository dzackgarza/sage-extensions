# #17: which generation statements cover ṼO⁺(N), and why none of them gives the kernel

N = U ⊕ U(2) ⊕ E8(−2), rank 12, signature (2, 10), the Enriques period lattice.  The unit
asked for a finite generating family of the stable kernel ṼO⁺(N) = ker(O⁺(N) → O(A_N)), at
the owner named by `_MISSING_ARITHMETIC_GENERATING_SET`, built from a published generation
statement whose hypothesis N actually meets.

**Result of the reading: no published statement gives generators of the kernel.**  One
published statement gives generators of the whole group O(N), and it is not the kernel: the
group isomorphism it goes through does not carry ṼO⁺(N) to the stable subgroup on the other
side, and the two stable subgroups have indices 46 998 591 897 600 and 2.  The statement
that covers N as a lattice (Dutour Sikirić–Hulek, Theorem 4.5) is an *algorithm* whose
output is the coset-expanded family the issue rules out, and its authors never generate the
kernel.  So the missing input is a theorem, not an implementation, and no call is added:
shipping one built on Kneser's conditions would ship a premise N does not satisfy.

## The statements, what each proves, and whether N meets it

### Kneser's generation theorem, as Gritsenko–Hulek–Sankaran quote it

V. Gritsenko, K. Hulek, G. K. Sankaran, *Abelianisation of orthogonal groups and the
fundamental group of modular varieties*, arXiv:0810.1614v1.

- **Theorem 1.1 (p. 4)**, citing M. Kneser, *Erzeugung ganzzahliger orthogonaler Gruppen
  durch Spiegelungen*, Math. Ann. 255 (1981), Satz 4: *L* even integral, Witt index ≥ 2
  over **R**, representing −2, with rank₃(L) ≥ 5 and rank₂(L) ≥ 6 ⟹ O′(L) is generated by
  the products σ_aσ_b of reflections in (−2)-vectors.
- N has Witt index 2 and represents −2, but **rank₂(N) = 2**, so the hypothesis fails.
- **Corollary 1.2 (p. 4)** carries the same conditions, so it fails with them.

### Gritsenko–Hulek–Sankaran, Proposition 3.4 (p. 12)

For L = U ⊕ U₁ ⊕ L₀ with *two* hyperbolic planes and the Kneser conditions:
ṼSO⁺(L) = O′(L) = E(L) = E_U(L₁) and ṼO⁺(L) = ⟨Γ_J(L₀), σ₁⟩.

This is the shape of statement this unit needs — an explicit, small family (the Jacobi group
of L₀ plus one reflection).  N does not have two hyperbolic planes, and cannot: a
U ⊕ U ⊆ N would be a unimodular rank-4 sublattice, which forces rank₂(N) ≥ 4, and
rank₂(N) = 2.  Their **Proposition 5.6 (p. 17)** says so in their own words:

> "The moduli space E is associated with the lattice L = U(2) ⊕ U ⊕ E8(−2), which has
> 2-rank 2 and therefore does not satisfy the Kneser conditions.  But E = O⁺(L)\D_L is also
> equal to O⁺(L′)\D_L′, where L′ = U ⊕ U(2) ⊕ E8(−1) … Since L′ does satisfy the Kneser
> conditions, Theorem 1.3 tells us that ṼO⁺(L′) is generated by pairs of reflections …"

so what they prove generation *for* is the commensurable lattice L′ = U ⊕ U(2) ⊕ E8(−1),
whose 2-rank is 10 (computed below), and the proposition is a statement about simply
connectedness, not about the stable subgroup of N.

### Gritsenko–Hulek, the group isomorphism O(N) ≅ O(2U ⊕ D8(−1))

V. Gritsenko, K. Hulek, *Moduli of polarized Enriques surfaces*, arXiv:1502.02723,
**equations (1)–(2), printed page 4**:

> N^∨(2) ≅ U ⊕ U(2) ⊕ E8(−1) ≅ 2U ⊕ D8(−1)   (1)
> O(N) ≅ O(N^∨) ≅ O(N^∨(2)) ≅ O(2U ⊕ D8(−1))   (2)

This is a genuine and useful statement: 2U ⊕ D8(−1) *does* satisfy the Kneser conditions,
so by GHS Proposition 3.4 the group O(N) has the small explicit generating family
⟨Γ_J(D8(−1)), σ₁⟩, transported through (1).  But **it is a statement about O(N), not about
ṼO⁺(N)**, and the isomorphism does not carry one to the other:

| lattice | rank₂ | \|A_L\| | \|O(A_L)\| = index of ṼO(L) in O(L) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N = U ⊕ U(2) ⊕ E8(−2) | 2 | 1024 = 2^10 | **46 998 591 897 600** = 2^21·3^5·5^2·7·17·31 |
| L′ = N^∨(2) ≅ 2U ⊕ D8(−1) | 10 | 4 = 2^2 | **2** |

An isomorphism of groups taking Ṽ(N) onto Ṽ(L′) would force those two indices to be equal.
They differ by thirteen orders of magnitude, so the family Kneser gives on the L′ side is a
generating family of O(N) whose elements have no reason to lie in Ṽ(N) — and most of them,
being the reflection σ₁ and the Jacobi transvections of a lattice with discriminant (Z/2)²,
do not: the kernel of O(N) → O(A_N) is the *level-2 discriminant* subgroup, which on the L′
side is far larger.

### Dutour Sikirić–Hulek, the only statement that covers N as a lattice

M. Dutour Sikirić, K. Hulek, *Moduli of polarised Enriques surfaces — computational
aspects*, arXiv:2302.01679v2, J. London Math. Soc. 110 (2024).

- **Lemma 4.2 (p. 28)**, with Scattone's *Proposition 3.7.3*: *U ⊕ U ⊕ L* has an
  approximate model — the generators of SL₂(Z)² and the Eichler transvections E_{e_i,v_ij}.
  The hypothesis is two hyperbolic planes, so N fails it; the paper's escape is the remark
  before §4.4 (p. 30) that N = U + U(2) + E8(−2) *embeds* in U + U + E8(−2), so the ambient
  approximate model is used and "no additional work is needed".
- **Theorem 4.5 (p. 30)**: *there exist algorithms solving the equivalence and automorphism
  group problems for integral non-degenerate lattices with r(L) ≥ 5.*  N has r(N) = 12, so
  this **does** cover N, and its proof is the one published route to generators of O(N) for
  our lattice: induct on s(L), use the approximate model's oracle at a splitting integer,
  Orth(s−1) on v₁^⊥, Alg 1 (p. 26) to cut O(v₁^⊥) down to the subgroup preserving L, and
  Alg 2 to transport along each oracle representative — "by taking those transformations
  when they exist and a generating set of H we actually find a generating set of O(L)".
- That family is the **coset-expanded** one: one transporter per representative of every
  orbit the oracle lists, recursively down to s = 1.  It is not the small structural family
  the issue needs, and it is a generating set of O(L), not of the kernel.
- DSH never generate the kernel, and say how they avoid it: **Lemma 3.6 (p. 22)** — for
  U ⊂ K, H ≤ G with U = ṼO(N) normal, double coset decompositions of G = O(N) by (K, H)
  correspond to those of G/U by (K/U, H̄), so the orbit splitting runs in the finite quotient
  O(N)/ṼO(N) ≅ O⁺(F₂^10) and no generating family of the kernel is ever built.

### Scattone / the issue body's candidate route

F. Scattone, *On the group of automorphisms of Enriques surfaces*, Proposition 3.7.3, as DSH
quote it in Lemma 4.2 (p. 28): the hypothesis is U ⊕ U.  N has one unscaled hyperbolic
plane, so the hypothesis fails as stated, exactly as the issue body says.

## The missing input, named

What the unit needs is a generation theorem for **ṼO⁺(N) itself** — the level-2 stable
subgroup of a 2-elementary lattice of signature (2, 10) whose 2-rank is 2 and which contains
no second hyperbolic plane.  Two shapes are precedented, and neither covers N:

1. **The analogue of GHS Proposition 3.4(16) with one hyperbolic plane.**
   GHS prove ṼSO⁺(L) = E(L) = ⟨t(e, a), t(f, a) : a ∈ L₁⟩ for L with two hyperbolic planes;
   for Λ_{2t} = 2U ⊕ ⟨−2t⟩, which fails the Kneser conditions only in rank₂ (their
   Example 1.4, p. 5), they establish the analogous identity by a separate argument
   (**Theorem 5.7 and (20), p. 18**), using the paramodular group and the elementary
   divisor theorem.  That argument is about 2U ⊕ ⟨−2t⟩ and does not
   transfer to N, whose second summand U(2) has discriminant (Z/2)² and no unimodular
   isotropic vector: the transvections E(N) are generated by the *unimodular* cusp Ze ⊂ U,
   and the 528 cusps of ṼO⁺(N) (DSH Table 2, row 87, #I₁ = 528) show a single transvection
   family cannot move every primitive isotropic vector onto e.
2. **Kneser's principal congruence theorem.**  GHS **p. 13** cite [Kn2, 11.4] as "the
   positive solution of the principal congruence problem for the spinorial kernel
   O′(L) = ṼSO⁺(L) for a lattice L with real Witt index ≥ 2" — a hypothesis N meets.  But it
   is a statement about *principal congruence* subgroups, and Ṽ(N) is not one:
   ker(O(N) → O(N/2N)) ⊆ Ṽ(N), because an isometry fixing N/2N pointwise fixes N^∨/N (the
   map x ↦ 2x embeds N^∨/N into N/2N), and the inclusion is proper — Ṽ(N) asks for
   triviality only on that 10-dimensional radical of the 12-dimensional N/2N.  Closing the
   gap between the congruence subgroup and Ṽ(N) is the missing step.

Either route needs a proof that is not in the literature I can read, so the unit stops here
with the obstruction named rather than a call built on a hypothesis N does not meet.

## What is not claimed and not done

No call is added at `_MISSING_ARITHMETIC_GENERATING_SET`, no test is added, and no generating
family is asserted.  Nothing here says ṼO⁺(N) is not finitely generated — it is, being an
arithmetic subgroup of a Q-group of Q-rank 2 — only that no statement I can cite generates
it, and that the two quoted families do not.

The `O(N) ≅ O(2U ⊕ D8(−1))` route is left standing and usable for what it does give: a small
explicit generating family of **O(N)**, via GHS Proposition 3.4 applied to 2U ⊕ D8(−1) and
transported through Gritsenko–Hulek (1).  That is not this issue's acceptance (which needs
ṼO⁺(N)), and it is not shipped here either, because its transport needs an explicit lattice
isometry N^∨(2) → 2U ⊕ D8(−1) that this tree does not yet construct.
