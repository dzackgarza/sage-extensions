# #285: the route at its owner, and the two cusps it now returns at

**Result: Vinberg's algorithm on the roots `G_v` admits, with the stop on his
finite-volume criterion, returns the published wall count at two of Sterk's five
cusps and does not return at two others.**  The change is at the owner of the
walk, not at the call site: the candidate enumeration asks the subgroup test
last, and `cusp_chamber` now accepts the controlling vector with the checks that
make it a point of `A(N)`.

## 1. The algorithm, and the statements it rests on

For a discrete group \(\Theta\) of motions of Lobachevskii space and \(\Gamma\)
the subgroup generated by all reflections in it, E. B. Vinberg,
*On the groups of unit elements of certain quadratic forms*, Math. USSR Sb. 16
(1972), §3.2 (pp. 27–29), takes a point \(p_0\), the normals \(e\) with
\((e,e)>0\) and \(R_e\in\Theta\), and lists them by the rule (40)
\((e,e_i)\le 0\) for every earlier term, in increasing distance from \(p_0\)
(formula (43)).  Here \(\Theta=G_v\) and the normals are the roots of \(N\) whose
reflection lies in \(G_v\) — `admits_root`.

- **Proposition 4, p. 28.**  \(P=\bigcap_i\Pi^-_{e_i}\), the polyhedron cut by
  the normals listed, is the cell of \(\Gamma\) at \(p_0\).  So every accepted
  root is a wall of the chamber and no accepted root is spurious.
- **Proposition 5, p. 29.**  If \(\mu(P^{(m)})<\infty\) the sequence breaks off at
  the \(m\)-th term and \(P^{(m)}=P\).  So the stop is the finite-volume
  criterion, and when it fires the walls found are all of them: no separate
  certificate of completeness is needed, and no bound belongs to the route.
- **The criterion on the diagram.**  E. B. Vinberg, *Hyperbolic reflection
  groups*, Russian Math. Surveys 40 (1985), §4.5, Theorem 4.1 (p. 54) states
  finite volume for an acute-angled polyhedron in terms of the critical
  principal submatrices of its Gram matrix, and §5.3 (pp. 59–60) reads the same
  statement on the Coxeter scheme: a vertex at infinity is a subscheme of rank
  \(n-1\) whose connected components are all parabolic (Table 2), and the
  polyhedron has finite volume exactly when those subschemes generate the whole
  extended complex.  The engine tests the geometric form, that every vertex cut
  out so far lies in the closure of the light cone.
- **The controlling vector.**  Sterk, *Compactifications of the period space of
  Enriques surfaces, Part I*, Math. Z. 207 (1991), §4.3.5, p. 19, takes
  \(x=e'+f'\) at the cusp \(e\), singles out the roots orthogonal to it, and
  minimizes with "the restriction that the reflection \(s_{e_{10}}\) belongs to
  \(F_e\)" — the same filter.  His three heights come out of the library's
  transcription of his roots, \(b(x,\alpha)^2/\lvert q(\alpha)\rvert=4,16,64\)
  (`results/issue285-cusp-chamber-step.md` §1).

## 2. What changed at the owner

**The candidate enumeration asks the subgroup test last.**
`_FilteredVinAl.IsNewRoot` is `VinAl.IsNewRoot`'s conjunction — not a repeat, a
root, beyond every accepted wall, in the subgroup — with the subgroup test moved
to the end.  The conjunction is the same, so the accepted set is the same; what
changes is what gets asked.  A question costs about 0.116 s of CPU (a
discriminant image, a product in \(O(A_L)\), a reflection of \(T_{En}\), a sift),
and the wall condition costs a handful of rank-ten integer inner products, so
asked first the filter answers for every root the enumeration reaches, while
asked last it answers only for those inside the chamber being built.

Measured on the module that exercises both shapes of this walk,
`tests/lattices/test_the_subgroup_cusp_chamber.sage`: **2 passed in 22.52 s**
(the filtered test of that module was 399.0 s of wall time in
`results/issue312-membership-sift.md` §3).

**`cusp_chamber` takes the controlling vector.**  It is accepted as a vector of
the ambient lattice (or of an equal copy, read coordinate-wise as
`root_reflection` reads a root), refused when it moves \(v\) and when it is not a
point of \(A(N)\), and projected to its class in \(N\).  Exercised end to end at
`Sterk_2`:

    Sterk_2 with x = e + f (square 2, orthogonal to v): 10 walls, complete True, 127.0s
    Sterk_2 with the engine's own point:                10 walls, complete True,  38.9s
    refused a vector that moves v: ... is not orthogonal to e_2 ...
    refused an isotropic vector: ... is not a point of the hyperbolic space ...

## 4. Why the other two do not return

The engine enumerates *every* solution of the norm equation in a layer of its
decomposition and only then applies the wall condition.  The number of
candidates is therefore set by the height of the farthest wall of the chamber
being built, and at `Sterk_1` that wall is the third one Sterk lists, at height
64 from \(x=e'+f'\).  The candidates at that height lie in one shell of the
definite lattice \(x^\perp\): \(w=d\,e-a\,x\) with \(a=b(e,x)\), \(d=q(x)\),
\(q(w)=d(d\,q(e)-a^2)\), which for \(a=16\), \(q(e)=-4\) is the vectors of square
1088 in a rank-nine lattice of determinant 256; the neighbouring shell of square
1056 **exceeded the 6 GiB resident guard** without returning a list.

Vinberg's own prescription is the opposite of what the engine does — §4.1, p. 30:

> "Construction of the sequence (38) thus reduces to writing down those solutions
> of the Diophantine equation (57) that satisfy conditions (58) in increasing
> order of the quantity (59), and in checking conditions (49) for each solution
> as it is written down."

Conditions (58) are the cone conditions against the normals already accepted.
Constraining the enumeration by them is what keeps the count small, and it is the
one step the engine does not take.  It is the remaining unit of this issue; with
it the two cusps that do not return would become reachable, and the check is the
same five-row test that is red now
(`tests/lattices/test_the_isotropic_reduction_image.sage`,
`test_every_cusp_chamber_has_the_wall_count_of_its_card_row`).

Since two of the five rows do not reproduce, `just test-push` was **not** run on
this commit: the tree is not green.
