For a chart U and a compact closed coordinate ball K contained in U, the represented Hausdorff topology supplies finite-stage separation of closed rational chart boxes. True is a certificate of disjoint images of the valid portions of the boxes, not a numerical guess. At any pair of distinct points, sufficiently small coordinate neighborhoods are separated uniformly at sufficiently large effort. A negative name of the closed diagonal supplies this certificate: enumerate open rational rectangle pairs disjoint from the diagonal; at stage n test containment in the first n rectangles. Conversely these local certificates enumerate an open complement of the diagonal.

For a point q, each finite compact search encloses its coordinates and the center of K by outward rational intervals. The initial box contains K. Splitting a box preserves its union. A discarded box either has a strict squared lower distance from the center exceeding the squared radius, or is certified disjoint from q by the topology. Exhausting the finite tree proves q outside K. If q is outside K, the open complement of the diagonal contains {q} times K; compactness gives uniform separating neighborhoods in the source and home coordinates, and a finite bound on effort. The rational enclosures converge and the longest-side subdivision mesh tends to zero, so eventually all boxes meeting K are certified and all boxes bounded away from K are discarded. This proves the exterior semidecision.

The bump evaluator alternates this search with finite-stage attempts to read the actual coordinate change, increasing the precision of the nested real predicate evaluations. When the point is in U, the positive search uses the actual overlap and smooth transition jets; when it is outside K, the zero jet is correct because the support is closed and contained in K. These branches agree when both apply. A zero guard is never inferred from failure to establish nonzero. A division whose denominator is not yet certified nonzero is distinguished from an actual zero denominator. The sphere's separator uses the injective embedding (2x/(1+|x|²), ±(1−2/(1+|x|²))) and outward interval enclosures; unequal images have at least one strictly separated coordinate. The public test of the translated coordinate chart independently supplies rational interval certificates for its actual stereographic embedding. Both value and first derivative are checked outside the support; the translated chart test also checks both on the overlap.

This algorithm is total on the appropriate represented data: a computably Hausdorff atlas with a complete separation representation and effective positive overlap transition jets and certified local radii. Mere abstract Hausdorffness, partial lists of transitions, or an evaluation-only callable do not supply these effective data; see the counterexample in the original issue comment 7245934225. The standard sphere's built-in certificates apply to the standard charts and their identity coordinate restrictions. Other coordinate presentations may supply their own topology data; the new API permits this without replacing the manifold, the classical function algebra, or the input derivation. The current arbitrary-atlas constructor still needs to supply complete effective data rather than rely on the existing partial registrations. In particular the remaining claim of full original acceptance cannot follow from the two tests or the conditional theorem alone.

Reference: Pauly, On the topological aspects of the theory of represented spaces, Proposition 6.2, Computability 5 (2016), https://doi.org/10.3233/COM-150049. The compact-cover and two-search arguments above are direct.
