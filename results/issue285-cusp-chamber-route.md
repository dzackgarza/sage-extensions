## 1. What the target is, and the two statements it rests on

`W = W(N)` is the reflection group of `N`, whose closed chamber Vinberg's
algorithm returns in 3.3 s of CPU with ten walls.  `W′ = ⟨s_r : r a root of N,
s_r ∈ G_v⟩` is the reflection subgroup that `admits_root` cuts out, and Sterk's
cusp diagram is its chamber.

- V. V. Deodhar, *A note on subgroups generated by reflections in Coxeter
  groups*, Arch. Math. (Basel) 53 (1989), no. 6, 543–546: the subgroup generated
  by any set of reflections of a Coxeter group is a Coxeter group with respect to
  a canonical set of generators.
- M. Dyer, *Reflection subgroups of Coxeter systems*, J. Algebra 135 (1990),
  57–73 — read here in the author's thesis (Univ. of Sydney, 1987), which states
  the same results with its own numbering: **(1.6.1), p. 7**,
  `S(W′) = { t ∈ T | N(t) ∩ W′ = {t} }`; **(1.8) Theorem, p. 9**,
  `(W′, S(W′))` is a Coxeter system; **(3.9) Theorem, p. 37**, a set of positive
  roots is `S(W′)` exactly when its pairwise normalised inner products lie in
  `{−cos(π/n)} ∪ (−∞,−1]`; **(3.15), p. 41**, the algorithm that computes
  `S(W′)` from a finite generating set of reflections by replacing a pair by the
  canonical generators of the dihedral group it generates.

`N(t)` is the set of reflections `u ∈ T` with `ℓ(ut) < ℓ(t)`; for `t = r_α`,
`N(r_α) = { r_β : β ∈ Φ⁺, β ≠ α, b(β,α) > 0 }` (the inversions of a reflection),
so Dyer's criterion reads:

    α ∈ S(W′)  ⟺  r_α is admitted, and no admitted β ∈ Φ⁺ with b(β,α) > 0.

Both halves are decided by the sift of #312.  What is missing is a **finite**
way to range over the candidates `α` and over the `β` that must be excluded.

## 2. The three routes, and what each does

**Vinberg with the filter** (`fundamental_chamber(admits_root=…)`, what #297
landed): at `Sterk_1`, 1400 membership questions in 964 s of wall time (360 s of
CPU) and no closure; at `max_decompositions=400`, no return in 2200 s.  The
unrestricted chamber of the same `N` closes in 3.3 s of CPU with ten walls, of
which the filter admits nine.  Asking it to stop after twelve *accepted* roots
(`max_roots=12`, unbounded decompositions) had not reached twelve after 14
minutes of wall time — so the height order the engine picks for the controlling
vector does not even present Sterk's twelve walls early.

**Growing the convex region from the closed chamber of `W`.**  Start from `C`,
the ten-wall chamber of `W`; a wall whose reflection is admitted is a facet of
`D`, and a wall whose reflection is not admitted is interior, so the region
extends across it: `R ↦ R ∪ s_α(R)`.  Implemented with exact cone arithmetic
(Sage `Cone`, extreme rays for redundancy).  It does not converge, and the
reason is structural, not a coding slip: **`R ∪ s_α(R)` is convex only when the
angle at the shared facet is at least a right angle.**  In `A2` — `W = S3`,
`W′ = ⟨r_2⟩`, `D` a half-space — the 60° chamber grows to a 120° cone, and the
next reflection across one of its facets produces 240°, which is not an
intersection of half-spaces at all; the half-space representation then returns a
strictly *smaller* region and the iteration wanders.  Measured at `Sterk_1`:
facet counts 10, 11, 11, 12, 11, 13, 12, 13, 12, 13, 13, 14, 11, … over sixty
steps, with the number of admitted facets falling as well as rising
(8, 9, 8, 9, 8, 9, 7, 8, 9) — which a monotone growth cannot do, confirming that
the representation is the failure and not the arithmetic.

**Walking the chambers inside `D`.**  Breadth-first over the chambers of `W`,
crossing a wall exactly when its reflection is not admitted, and collecting the
admitted walls met.  Measured at `Sterk_1`: the closed chamber of `W` has ten
walls of which **nine** are admitted; after **120** chambers the admitted walls
are still those nine, and the queue holds 26 more.  So the three further walls of
Sterk's diagram are not within 120 chambers of `C`, and the walk gives no
termination certificate.

## 3. What the numbers say about the group

Since every admitted reflection is a reflection of `N` and `W` contains them all,
`W′ ≤ W`; `D ⊇ C` is a union of `W`-chambers.  The walk not reaching three facets
within 120 chambers means either `[W : W′] = ∞` — in which case no chamber-level
algorithm can enumerate `D` — or those facets are far from `C`.  Either way the
route has to work at the level of *facets* (Dyer's criterion above), not of
chambers, and it has to make the inner condition `b(β,α) > 0` finite.

The candidates for a finite inner search are the admitted roots themselves; the
library can decide membership of any one of them, but nothing measured here bounds
how far the search must go, and a bound is not a route.
