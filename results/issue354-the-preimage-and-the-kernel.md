# #354: the preimage call is blocked at the kernel's generating set

**Superseded below.** The call the issue asks for is `rho_L^{-1}(P)`'s generators, and the route
this section records — lifts of `P`'s generators together with generators of `ker rho_L` — cannot
be completed: the second part has no owner in this library, and the owner says so in a named
constant. What this section verifies is the first half's warrant and the failure's precise site;
the section at the end replaces the route and the call now returns generators.

## No surjectivity theorem is used, per the batch's correction

Nikulin's Theorem 1.14.2 needs `rank L >= l(A_L) + 2`. Card `0125` is 2-elementary with
`a = 10 = rank`, so the theorem does not apply there and it is not used. What is used instead
is the image of `O(L)`, computed from `O(L)`'s own generators:

```
|O(q)|:        46998591897600   in 10.43s (3.04 s on an idle run)
|rho(O(M))|:   46998591897600   in 12.14s ( 5.02 s idle, then 0.12 s to read the order)
equal, so the image of O(M) in O(q) is all of O(q)
```

`|rho(O(M))|` is `M.discriminant_image()`: the subgroup generated by the selected generators of
`O(M)` mapped through `rho_M`. It equals `|O(q)|`, so `im(rho) = O(q)` and `im(rho) ∩ P = P` for
the decomposition stabilizer `P <= O(q)` — the intersection is therefore `P` itself, and it is
`P`'s order that the acceptance compares with the order of the returned generators' image.

The lattice's discriminant picture, measured on the way:

```
A_M: ten invariant factors, all 2  ->  |A_M| = 1024, l(A_M) = 10
rank 10, signature (1, 9), determinant -1024
isotropic classes of q_M: 528 of 1024 (15.55 s)
A_U(2) inside A_M = {0, [0], [1], [0]+[1]}, from the primitive duals of U(2)'s basis
```

## The blocker: the kernel has no generating set

`ker rho_M = Õ(M)`, `M.stable_orthogonal_group()` (`lattices.py:4199`). Its generators, if asked:

```
stable_orthogonal_group():    0.00s
group_generators():           no return within 240 s (probe stopped)
```

because they are constructed by Schreier's lemma on the coset space `O(L)/ker rho` =
`O(q)`, of order 46 998 591 897 600. That is the observed failure of the issue restated
precisely: not a slowness in the transporter but a Schreier family over `4.7e13` cosets.

The owner states the gap itself, and the name is the one to work against:

* `src/sage_extensions/categories/orthogonal_quotients.py:1854` —
  `_MISSING_ARITHMETIC_GENERATING_SET = ("Over an indefinite lattice L, O(L) is an infinite
  arithmetic group and no generating set of this subgroup is known.  The missing operation is a
  generating set for an arithmetic subgroup of O(L), which belongs in lattice_engines.py")`.
* `src/sage_extensions/categories/orthogonal_quotients.py:776`,
  `OrthogonalCharacterQuotient.subgroup_generator_lifts`, whose docstring ends (lines 800--805):
  "This is the presented generator family whose discriminant images a consumer can build itself;
  it contains no claim about generating `Gamma` itself (the stable kernel of `O(L) -> O(A_L)`
  inside the subgroup is needed for that: an open capability of the lattice-engine owner, named
  by `_MISSING_ARITHMETIC_GENERATING_SET`)."

So the lifts of `P`'s generators exist and are cheap — `Lattices...discriminant_lift`
(`lattice_morphisms.py`, "Return `g` in `O(L)` inducing `automorphism`", exact, `None` when
there is none) — but the set they form does not generate `rho^{-1}(P)`: it misses `Õ(M)`
entirely. Returning it as "generators of `G_e`" would be the wrong object, and a test that
compares `|rho(<lifts>)|` with `|P|` would pass on it while the group were wrong, since every
kernel element is invisible to `rho`.

## What is therefore still owed

1. A generating set for an arithmetic subgroup of `O(L)` at `lattice_engines.py` — the named
   missing operation. For `M(2)` the candidates worth checking against a printed statement are
   the Eichler transvections `t(c, a)` of Gritsenko--Hulek--Sankaran, arXiv:1012.4155v2, §8.3,
   equation (49) (defined there for `c` isotropic and `a` in `c^perp`); that survey states their
   properties but no generation theorem, so the citation that they generate `Õ(L)` is not yet in
   hand. The library's own `eichler_transvection` (`isotropic_parabolics.py:501`) and
   `complement_eichler_transvections` (`eichler_criterion.py:305`) are built for `2U + K`
   Eichler models, and `M(2)` contains no `U` summand.
2. The order of the decomposition stabilizer, computed in `O(q)`. Two routes were tried and are
   not yet measured: walking the `O(q)`-set of 2-dimensional subspaces from `A_{U(2)}` (a BFS
   over `O(q)`'s four generators that had not returned in ten minutes), and counting the
   subspaces of that isometry type directly, which the probe attempted and lost to a comparison
   defect (`form.q(point) == form.zero()` is not the right zero for a `Q/2Z`-valued form), so no
   number is claimed.

`A_{U(2)}` inside `A_M` is computable and was computed: the primitive duals of the `U(2)` basis
vectors give classes `[0]` and `[1]` of order two, so `A_{U(2)} = {0, [0], [1], [0]+[1]}` in
`A_M`'s coordinates, consistent with `A_M = A_{U(2)} + A_{E8(-2)}` and `q(U(2)) = u(2)`.

## What the correction changes, and what it does not

The citation is withdrawn: nothing here rests on a surjectivity theorem, and the image is
computed instead. The blocker is untouched by that, because it sits in the *other* factor of the
route: `rho^{-1}(P)` is generated by lifts of `P`'s generators together with `ker rho`, and the
world in which the image is all of `O(q)` is exactly the world in which `ker rho` is large — it
is `O(L)`'s own kernel, whose generating set the library does not have.

One route was attempted towards it and is not ready to claim: the decomposition stabilizer's
order, needed as one side of the acceptance's comparison. Two ways to it were run and neither
returned a number.

* The `O(q)`-set walk from the 2-dimensional subspace `A_{U(2)}`: four generators applied to a
  four-element frozenset, which had not closed after ten minutes, so the orbit is large (it is
  `[O(q) : P]`).
* The direct count of 2-dimensional `u(2)` subspaces, `#{(x,y) : q(x)=q(y)=0, b(x,y) != 0}/2`,
  over the 528 isotropic classes: the owned module's `q` and `b` cost about 3 ms per evaluation,
  so `528^2 = 278 784` of them did not finish inside a 1200 s cap. The count itself is the right
  formula — the isotropic-class count it starts from reproduced the known 528 of 1024 — it is
  the per-evaluation cost of the owned discriminant module that makes it slow. Reading the
  form's Gram matrix over `F_2` once and counting by linear algebra would settle it, and is the
  next step if this number is wanted before the kernel's generating set exists.

## The stabilizer route: the call returns generators (this turn; supersedes everything above)

The kernel route above is not needed.  `O(L)` acts on `A_L` through `rho_L`, so `rho_L^-1(P)` is
the stabilizer of the *point* whose stabilizer in `O(A_L)` is `P`, and Schreier's lemma turns a
transversal of that point's orbit into generators of the stabilizer.  No generating set of
`ker rho_L` is required, and none is used.

**Citation.**  A. Seress, *Permutation Group Algorithms*, Cambridge Tracts in Mathematics 152,
Cambridge University Press, 2003, **Lemma 4.2.1, p. 57**: for `H <= G = <S>` and a right
transversal `R` of `G` mod `H` with `1 in R`, the set `{r s \overline{rs}^{-1} | r in R, s in S}`
generates `H`.  Its proof there is stated for infinite groups, which is this case.  The trivial
members are dropped by the sweep's own datum: Holt--Eick--O'Brien, *Handbook of Computational
Group Theory*, Chapman & Hall/CRC, 2005, **Section 4.1, p. 79** records that the Schreier
generator of the pair that first reaches a point is trivial by definition
(`t_(s.p) = s t_p`) and need not be appended.

**Measured on card `0125`**, `L = U(2) + E8(-2)` (Gram from the card; rank 10, signature (1,9),
`A_L = (Z/2)^10`):

```
q on the F2 basis of A_L            [1,1,0,0,1,1,0,0,0,0]       (q table read once, 7.0 s)
polar bits on the basis pairs       from q(x+y)^q(x)^q(y)       (0 mismatches vs owned b on 100 pairs)
A_U(2) masks                        0, 256, 512, 768            q = 0,0,0,1;  polar nondegenerate
|O(L)| generators                   15                          (2.1 s)
|orbit of A_U(2)| = [O(q):P]        67456                       (0.4 s on the mask model; 58.8 s through the G-set)
|O(q)|                              46998591897600
|P| = |O(q)| / 67456                696729600
|P| from the two summands           2 * 348364800 = 696729600   (|O(q_U(2))| = 2, |O(q_E8(-2))| = 348364800)
Schreier family                     15 * 67456 - 67455 = 944385 (the trivial tree pairs dropped)
rho_L onto                          |rho_L(O(L))| = |O(q)| = 46998591897600
peak RSS                            2135 MiB
```

The 67456 also equals the count of the point's isometry type by linear algebra alone --- the
ordered isotropic pairs with nonzero pairing, `134912 / 2` --- so the orbit search and the count
agree, and Witt's extension theorem is what makes them the same number.

**What `discriminant_preimage` now returns.**  `L.Aut().discriminant_preimage(P,
orbit_point=...)` returns the same predicate subgroup as before, with its selected generating
morphism replaced by the Schreier family of the point's orbit: 944 385 isometries, one per
(point, letter) pair except the `67455` pairs that first reach a point.  Values are computed on
demand from the transversal words (0.000--0.097 s each); the family is not materialized as a
list.  `L.Aut().discriminant_point_orbit(point)` returns the orbit itself as a finite `O(L)`-set,
whose cardinality is the index.

**The public calls.**  `L.Aut().discriminant_point_stabilizer(point)` returns `rho_L^-1` of the
point's stabilizer --- `G_e` on `0125` --- and `L.Aut().discriminant_preimage(P,
orbit_point=point)` does the same when `P` is already in hand.  The test
`tests/lattices/test_the_decomposition_preimage_returns_generators.sage` asserts, from computed
values only: `2 * 348364800 * 67456 = 46998591897600` (`|P| * |orbit| = |O(q)|`, `|P|` read from
the two summands), `|rho_L(O(L))| = |O(q)|`, the family's cardinality `= 15 * 67456 - 67455`,
and that five sampled members lie in the preimage and preserve the point under the preimage's own
`preimage_morphism()`.  `1 passed in 52.65 s` at commit `15a3d13` (log:
`.tmp/qc-evidence/issue354-preimage-test.log`).

**What is not claimed.**  The order of the image of the whole family in `O(q)` was not computed:
the family has 944 385 members, and no route to an order of `696729600` inside `O(q)` from that
family was run.  The image is reached instead through `rho_L` being onto `O(q)` together with
`P`'s being the point's stabilizer.  An earlier version of the action keyed its class
permutations by object identity without retaining the objects; a reused `id` then handed the
action another generator's permutation and the orbit silently collapsed to 4608 points (family
size 64513), which the test caught.  The table now retains its elements; the collapse is gone.
Values are also checked through `preimage_morphism()` rather than
`generator.discriminant_morphism()`, which disagrees with `rho_L(generator)` on some deeper
family members --- a separate observation about that accessor, not investigated here.

**Costs.**  The `F_2` model (`_DiscriminantF2Model`) is read once per call: `form.elements()`
plus the span closure 0.4 s, `q` on the 1024 classes 7.0 s, one permutation of the classes per
generator 4 s.  `pointed_orbit` over the 67456-point orbit 34 s, `schreier_generating_morphism`
15 s, the whole `group_generators()` 21 s.  `preimage.discriminant_image()` (the character
quotient route) had not returned after 300 s and is not used.
